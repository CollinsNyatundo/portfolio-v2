## Problem

The GEO-AI challenge asked teams to label points in two regions, Fergana in Uzbekistan and Orenburg in Russia, as cropland or not, using Sentinel-1 radar and Sentinel-2 optical satellite data. Maps like this help plan food security and land use where field surveys are expensive.

## Constraints

- **Few labels.** 1,000 labelled points, about 30% cropland.
- **Large, messy imagery.** About 1.75 million radar rows and 5.6 million optical rows, with clouds and gaps.
- **Two very different regions.** A model that learns one landscape can fail on the other.
- **A team and a deadline.** Five contributors over a few weeks.

## Approach

I led a team of five. We aggregated the satellite rows by location and matched each labelled point to its nearest pixels with a BallTree search, falling back to rounded coordinates where coverage was thin. On top of the raw bands we computed vegetation and water indices (NDVI, NDWI, EVI, SAVI and others) and a radar ratio, then selected the 40 most informative features.

We tuned RandomForest, XGBoost, LightGBM and CatBoost with Optuna, tried oversampling for the class imbalance, and stacked the models into an ensemble with a small neural network on top.

## Evaluation

The final comparison holds out a stratified random 20% of the labelled points, 200 of them, and scores every model on that same split. The figure that matters most is the baseline: labelling every point "non-crop" is already right 70.3% of the time.

## Results

RandomForest reached 0.745 accuracy on the held-out points, about 4 points above always guessing "non-crop". The stacked ensemble scored 0.725, below the single model it was built from.

<Callout title="What I'd tell my past self">
  Some earlier runs scored around 0.76, but they oversampled before splitting,
  so synthetic neighbours of test points sat in training. And random splits let
  nearby fields leak across. Both make a model look better than it is. The
  honest figure is the modest one.
</Callout>

## Security

The data is public satellite imagery and labelled coordinates, with no personal data. The project ships trained models as joblib files; anyone reusing them should load them only from a trusted copy, because joblib files can run code when loaded.

## What I'd do next

- Evaluate with spatial blocks or region holdout, so the score reflects new land.
- Oversample inside each training fold only.
- Add seasonal features from the time series, which the notes flagged and we didn't build.
- Report intervals, not single numbers, on a split this small.
